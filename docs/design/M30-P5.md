# M30 Part 5: several volumes of one filesystem type at once (design)

**Scope.** Today there is one `CoreDriver` per disk filesystem type (`src/kernel/filesystem/drivers.cpp`: `disk_driver`, `ext2_driver`, `ext3_driver`, `ext4_driver`). `CoreDriver::Mount(type, disk, mode)` keeps the first partition the type probes, and `VFS::MountBlockDevice` registers four candidates, one per type. So with the ext4 root on `vda1`, a second ext4 volume is never opened. One `file_system_lock` (`src/kernel/files.cpp`) serializes every filesystem operation.

Already per instance:

- `TypeRegistry::Find(type, source)`;
- `MountTable` (`Holds`, `DeviceOf`, users);
- `FileKey {driver, node}` and the per-mount counts in `disk_files`;
- `/proc/mounts` (`DescribeMount`);
- the core: its `FilesystemType` objects hold no state, and every core `Mount` allocates its own filesystem with its own `BlockCache` and `Host::Lock`, so the core lock is already per volume.

This part:

1. Teaches `Partition::Find` MBR extended partitions, with Linux's numbering.
2. Gives each partition its own driver.
3. Gives the test disk a second ext4 volume in both layouts, mounted beside the ext4 root in every STORAGE run.
4. Replaces the global filesystem lock with one lock per volume.

**Done when:**

1. `./build.sh unit-test` and `./build.sh unit-test-tsan` (new);
2. `bash tools/quality.sh check`;
3. `tools/check.sh --arch both --modes "SHELL STORAGE"`;
4. `tools/check.sh --arch both --matrix`.

## Decisions (confirmed by the user, 2026-11-20)

### Partitions

- **D1. MBR extended partitions in `Partition::Find`.**
  - The first primary entry of type 0x05, 0x0F or 0x85 is the extended container; later extended entries are ignored.
  - The chain starts at the container's first sector. In each EBR:
    - entry 0 is the logical, its start relative to **this EBR's** sector;
    - entry 1 links to the next EBR, its start relative to the **container's** first sector (Linux's convention);
    - entries 2 and 3 are ignored.
  - Logicals are listed under the same type rule as primaries: 0x06, 0x0E or 0x83, with a non-zero count.
  - A protective MBR still means GPT, and GPT disks never walk EBRs.
- **D2. Linux numbering.**
  - `Partition::Range` gains `std::uint32_t number`:
    - MBR primaries are numbered by slot (1–4), and empty or unsupported slots leave a gap;
    - logicals are numbered from 5 in chain order;
    - GPT entries are numbered by entry index.
  - The extended container is not listed.
  - `vdaN` is that number everywhere: `PartitionSource`, `root=vdaN`, the registry and `/proc/mounts`. The pool slot is the number, so up to 16 numbered partitions are reachable; numbers above 16 are ignored.
- **D3. The test disk** grows from 160 to 166 MiB (339968 sectors). Both layouts hold the same volumes at the same sectors, so the harness constants don't depend on the layout:

  | Volume | Sectors (first..end) | Size | MBR | GPT | Name |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | root ext4 | 2048..262144 | 127 MiB | slot 1 | entry 1 | `vda1` |
  | FAT16 | 262144..294912 | 16 MiB | slot 2 | entry 2 | `vda2` |
  | ext3 | 294912..303104 | 4 MiB | slot 3 | entry 3 | `vda3` |
  | extended container | 303104..331776 | 14 MiB | slot 4, type 0x05 | entry 4 empty | (not listed) |
  | EBR 1 | 303104 | 1 sector | logical 1 → data ext4; link to EBR 2 at relative 10240, count 18432 | zero | |
  | data ext4 | 305152..313344 | 4 MiB | logical 1 | entry 5 | `vda5` |
  | EBR 2 | 313344 | 1 sector | logical 2 → ext2, no link | zero | |
  | ext2 | 315392..331776 | 8 MiB | logical 2 | entry 6 | `vda6` |
  | free | 331776..339968 | 4 MiB | free | free, but the backup table in the last 33 sectors | |

  - The logicals are 1 MiB-aligned (2048 sectors after their EBR), as `fdisk` places them.
  - **ext2 is renamed from `vda4` to `vda6`, and moves.** GPT entry 4 is left empty so GPT numbering matches MBR's. Every harness, kernel-test and documentation reference to ext2's name or sectors changes. `storage.py` and `power.py` import the constants.
  - The data volume is made with `mke2fs -t ext4 -b 1024 -I 256 -J size=1`, with UUID `EXT4_DATA_VOLUME_UUID` and GPT name "FEATHERDATA". It holds `/hello.txt` ("Hello from the second ext4 partition.\n") and `/samples/hello.txt` ("data volume\n"; the root has the same path with other contents).

### Per-volume drivers

- **D5. A static pool.** `constinit CoreDriver volume_drivers[16]`, and `Volume(n)` is `vdaN`'s driver for the kernel's whole life.
  - There is no heap allocation and no freeing, so a `FileKey.filesystem` never dangles or names another volume.
  - `CoreDriver` stores no `this` pointer, so GCC 14's zero-fill of self-referencing `constinit` arrays does not apply. If it ever did, the first virtual call at boot would crash; the fallback is placement-new once in `Volume()`.
- **D6. The kind comes from the volume.** `constexpr CoreKind Kinds[]` is tried in this order:
  1. `{Fat16Type, "fat16", {}}`
  2. `{Ext2Type, "ext2", {.periodic_sync = true}}`
  3. `{Ext3Type, "ext3", {true, true}}`
  4. `{Ext4Type, "ext4", {true, true}}`

  The first kind whose `Probe` passes and whose read-only core `Mount` succeeds wins. A probe that passes with a mount that fails falls through to the next kind. The options and the registry name are set when the volume opens.
- **D7. Eager discovery.**
  - At `MountBlockDevice` every partition is opened read-only in the core. A volume the core refuses stays unregistered (`ENOENT`/`ENODEV`).
  - An ext volume costs about 40 KiB.
  - FAT16 is also found read-only now, and reopens read-write at `PrepareMount`.
- **D8. A volume mounts at one point at most.** Mounting it again anywhere, the root included, gives `EBUSY`. Documented as a difference from Linux.
- **D9. Every kind is multi-instance:** FAT16, ext2, ext3 and ext4.
- **D10. `mount -t TYPE vdaN`** looks up `(type, PartitionSource(n))`.
  - A wrong variant for an existing partition gives `ENOENT`.
  - A type no volume has gives `ENODEV`.
- **D11. The per-type accessors go:** `Drivers::Disk/Ext2/Ext3/Ext4/MountDisk/MountExt2/3/4`. Their callers change:

  | Caller | Change |
  |---|---|
  | `vfs.cpp` | uses the pool |
  | `kernel_test.cpp` `FreeDiskBlocks` | `VolumeOf(VFS::MountedAt(FatPoint))->Statistics` |
  | `filesystem_tests.cpp`'s uses | `VolumeOf(VFS::MountedAt("/"))` |
  | `filesystem_tests.cpp`'s "core read-only again" `MountExt2()` | `VFS::Remount("/", ReadOnly)` |

- **D12. Root selection keeps its meaning.** `ChooseRoot` runs over the pool by number, and `root=vda5` works.
- **D13. Registry.**
  - `TypeRegistry::Capacity` goes from 16 to 32, with a `static_assert` that it is at least the built-ins plus 16 volumes.
  - `MountTable::Capacity` stays 16.
  - **Renaming a driver's source is dropped:** `Register` of a known driver under another name fails, and `MountBlockDevice` does `Unregister` then `Register` per slot.
- **D14. Writeback** walks mounts (`ForEachMount`), not types.

### Per-volume locking

- **D15. Locks, in acquisition order.** Nothing takes an earlier lock while holding a later one.
  1. **`namespace_lock`.** A new global `Sync::Mutex` in `files.cpp`, taken by `Files::Mount`, `Unmount` and `Remount`, `Quiesce`, and boot's `MountBlockDevice`.
  2. **One volume lock,** `Driver::VolumeLock()`, a `Sync::VolumeMutex` (D16):
     - each `CoreDriver` has its own;
     - `/tmp` and `/etc` share one `tmpfs_lock`, because they share Tmpfs's node table;
     - `/system`, `/dev` and `/proc` each have their own.
  3. **`table_lock`.** This is today's `file_system_lock`, renamed and narrowed to descriptor tables, `OpenFile` slots and references, and `disk_files`. It is never held across a driver call, nor while waiting for a volume lock.
  4. **`mount_lock`** (a spinlock, unchanged; tables only).
  5. **Leaf spinlocks:** the core's per-volume `Host::Lock`, the virtio `device_lock`, and the heap.
- **D16. `Sync::VolumeMutex`** is a `Mutex` that records its owner (`Scheduler::CurrentTaskId()`). The rule is that **a task holds at most one volume lock**, except in `Quiesce`.
  - `VFS::Use` and the handle operations lock the driver's volume lock unless the calling task already holds it.
  - Compound operations in `Files` take the lock once around the whole sequence.
  - Direct callers (kernel tests, `UseRootEtc`, host tests) get one lock per call.
  - Locking a second, different volume lock is `Panic("VFS: two volume locks")`. Host tests count it instead.
- **D17. What each operation takes.**
  - **Walks** (`Resolved::Walk`, `path_walk.cpp`, `ResolvePath`) lock each component's volume for that one driver call only, never across steps.
  - **Path operations** (`Open`, `Stat`, `Remove`, `MakeDirectory`, `RemoveDirectory`, `SymbolicLink`, `ReadLink`, `ChangeDirectory`, `CheckDirectory`, and the loader's `ProgramFile::Open`):
    1. walk with no lock;
    2. take `VolumeHold hold(resolved)`, the mount user and then the final path's volume lock;
    3. run the check-then-act sequence under it, nesting `table_lock` for `FreeSlot`, `AddUse`, `FindUse` and `DropUse`.
  - **`Rename` and `Link`:** walk both paths. `VFS::Locate` (under `mount_lock`) compares the drivers: different drivers give `EXDEV` before any lock is taken; otherwise one volume lock is taken.
  - **`ReadDirectory`:** indexes below `ChildCount(dir)` use the child mount's lock, the rest the directory's own; never both.
  - **Descriptor operations** (`Read`, `Write`, `Seek`, `StatDescriptor`, `ProgramFile::ReadAt`):
    1. under `table_lock`, pin the `OpenFile` (`references++`) and copy `handle.driver`;
    2. take that driver's volume lock and operate;
    3. under `table_lock`, unpin. If that was the last reference, the file is closed as in `Close`.
  - **`Close`, `CloseAll` and the last unpin:**
    1. under `table_lock`, clear the slot and drop the reference; reaching 0 takes ownership of the file;
    2. outside it, for a disk or tmpfs file: take the volume lock, then `table_lock` for `DropUse`, then `VFS::Close`.
  - **`Mount`:** `namespace_lock`, then the walk, then the volume lock for `PrepareMount`, then `mount_lock`.
  - **`Unmount`:** `namespace_lock`, then `BeginUnmount`, then the volume lock for `Detach`, then `mount_lock`.
  - **`Remount`:** `namespace_lock`, then the volume lock, then `table_lock` for the open-file count.
  - **`SyncAll` and `SyncPeriodic`:** no global lock. `ForEachMount` takes each mount's user and volume lock, one mount at a time.
  - **`Quiesce`:** `namespace_lock`, then every mounted driver's volume lock in mount order (`DetachAll`), then `table_lock`, all held for good. This cannot deadlock: every other task holds at most one volume lock and, while holding it, waits only for `table_lock`, `mount_lock` or leaf locks, which nobody holds while waiting for a volume lock.
  - **`/proc/mounts`** reads other volumes' `IsMountedReadOnly()` without their locks, so `Driver::mounted_read_only` becomes `std::atomic<bool>` (relaxed).
- **D18. The VFS error is per task.**
  - `VFS::LastError()` reads `Scheduler::CurrentLocal().filesystem_error`, from a new per-task struct with a boot slot.
  - `CoreDriver::last_error` stays per driver, read under its volume lock.
  - The host stub is `thread_local`.
- **D19. Annotations.**
  - A pseudo-capability `Sync::Role held_volume`. `VolumeHold` (`SCOPED_CAPABILITY`) does `ACQUIRE(held_volume)`.
  - `files.cpp` helpers that call drivers are `REQUIRES(held_volume)`, and `OpenFile::handle` is `GUARDED_BY(held_volume)`.
  - The walk, `ReadDirectory`, `ResolvePath` and the sync helpers are `EXCLUDES(held_volume)`.
  - Descriptor tables, slots, references and `disk_files` are `GUARDED_BY(table_lock)`.
  - `VolumeMutex::Lock`/`Unlock` and `Quiesce` are `NO_THREAD_SAFETY_ANALYSIS`.
  - clang-tidy stays clean.
- **D20. Out of scope:** device-level parallelism. The virtio queue still handles one sector at a time under `device_lock`.
- **D21. A ThreadSanitizer unit build.**
  - `./build.sh unit-test-tsan` configures `build/unit-tsan` with `FEATHEROS_THREAD_SANITIZER` (mutually exclusive with the ASan option) and runs the `[concurrency]` tests.
  - It is part of "done" for steps 4–5 and is run before the matrix at the end of the part.

## 2. Invariants

### Partitions (step 1)

- **I1. Termination.** Each EBR read is at a sector strictly after the previous one and inside the container, with at most 64 reads, so loops end. **Riskiest.**
- **I2. Bounds.** A logical is listed only if:
  - its type is supported and its count is non-zero;
  - its first sector is after its EBR;
  - it lies inside the container and the disk.

  An invalid next link (outside the container, at or before the current EBR, or of a non-extended type) ends the chain. **Riskiest.**
- **I3. Offsets.** A logical starts at its EBR's sector plus entry 0's start. The next EBR is at the container's first sector plus entry 1's start.
- **I4. Order and numbers.**
  - Primaries come in slot order with numbers 1–4, the container excluded.
  - Logicals follow in chain order, numbered 5, 6, …
  - GPT is numbered by entry index.
  - At most `ranges.size()` entries are stored.
  - Only the first extended entry is walked, and GPT walks no EBRs.
- **I5. Failure stays local.** A read error or a missing 0x55AA on an EBR ends the chain and keeps everything found so far.

### Volumes (step 2)

- **I6. Fixed drivers.** `Volume(n)` alone holds partition number `n`, and pool objects never die.
- **I7. Registration matches the last scan.** No stale `(type, vdaN)` survives, and 16 volumes plus the built-ins fit. **Riskiest.**
- **I8. One mount point per volume.** Each mount has its own `st_dev`.
- **I9. State stays per volume:** the mode, `core_read_only`, `last_error`, the journal, the cache and the options. Nothing done on one volume changes another. **Riskiest.**
- **I10. Write isolation.** Every sector `Volume(n)` writes lies in partition `n`'s range. **Riskiest.**
- **I11.** `SyncPeriodic` and `DetachAll` reach every mount, even after one fails.
- **I12. Open-file identity is per volume.** The same inode number on two volumes is two files. **Riskiest.**
- **I13. The options follow the kind.**

  | Kind | `periodic_sync` | `close_on_detach` |
  |---|---|---|
  | ext2 | yes | no |
  | ext3, ext4 | yes | yes |
  | FAT16 | no | no |

- **I14. Rescan.** A volume mounted at a non-root point keeps its core. Every other slot is reopened or forgotten.
- **I15.** A rename or link between two volumes gives `EXDEV`.

### Test disk (step 3)

- **I16.** Both layouts give the same numbers for the same volumes at the same sectors, and both ext4 volumes pass `e2fsck -fn` after each boot.

### Locking (steps 4–5)

- **I17.** The lock order is D15's.
- **I18. At most one volume lock per task**, except in `Quiesce`. The walk, `ReadDirectory`, `ResolvePath`, the syncs and `/proc` generation hold none. **Riskiest.**
- **I19. Independence.** An operation on volume A never waits for B's lock. **Riskiest.**
- **I20. Compound atomicity.** Each check-then-act sequence in `Files` runs under one hold of its volume's lock:
  - `FindUse` → `Remove`/`Rename`/`Link`;
  - `Open` → `Create` → `AddUse` → `O_TRUNC`;
  - the kind checks → `Rename`;
  - `Remount`'s count → the change.

  **Riskiest.**
- **I21.** `table_lock` and `mount_lock` are never held across a driver call or while waiting for a volume lock.
- **I22. Who guards what.**
  - Handle state changes only under its driver's volume lock.
  - Slots, references and `disk_files` change only under `table_lock`.
  - An `OpenFile` used outside `table_lock` is pinned.

  **Riskiest.**
- **I23.** `VFS::LastError()` is the calling task's.
- **I24.** Mount-tree changes are serialized by `namespace_lock`. `PrepareMount`, `Detach` and `Remount` run under the volume's lock.
- **I25.** `/tmp` and `/etc` share a lock, and every core volume has its own.
- **I26.** `Quiesce` ends holding `namespace_lock`, every mounted volume's lock and `table_lock`, after `DetachAll` reached every mount.

## 3. Edge cases and errno

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| The test disk, MBR | `vda1`–`vda3` primaries, `vda5` data ext4, `vda6` ext2; `vda4` does not exist (`ENOENT`) |
| An EBR chain that loops | the logicals before the loop are listed and the walk ends |
| A logical outside the container or the disk, with count 0, at its EBR, or of type 0x07 | skipped; later ones still listed |
| A link outside the container or of a non-extended type | the chain ends |
| An EBR without 0x55AA, or a failed read | the chain ends; earlier entries kept |
| Two extended entries | only the first is walked |
| A partition numbered above 16 | not reachable; `vda17` is `ENOENT` |
| An extended entry in a GPT protective MBR | not walked |
| `mount -t ext4 vda5 /data` with the ext4 root `vda1` read-write | 0; both in `/proc/mounts` |
| The same volume elsewhere, or `vda1` at `/x` | `EBUSY` |
| `mount -t ext3 vda5`, `vda9`, `vda17` | `ENOENT` |
| A type no partition has | `ENODEV` |
| A 17th mount | `ENOMEM` |
| `rename`/`link` from `/data` to `/` | `EXDEV`, decided before any volume lock |
| Writes fail inside `vda5` only | `/data` gives `EIO`, then `ro`; `/` stays read-write |
| `root=vda5` | the data volume is `/` |
| A walk crossing from `/` into `/data` | one volume lock per step |
| `/data` slow while `/` and `/tmp` are in use | `/` and `/tmp` finish without waiting |
| `Remove` racing an open on the same volume | either the open finishes first (`EBUSY`) or the removal does (`ENOENT`) |
| `read` racing `close` on one descriptor | the read finishes on the pinned file, or gives `EBADF` |
| Shutdown during I/O on two volumes | `Quiesce` waits for each lock, then detaches |

## 5. Tests

### Step 1 (`src/fs/core/tests/partition_tests.cpp`)

**Oracle.** macOS has no tool that writes or walks EBR chains (BSD `fdisk` and `mpartition` handle primaries only; `sfdisk` and `parted` are absent). So each test builds its EBRs with `WriteEbr` from **raw** relative fields, and compares `Find`'s output with **literal, hand-computed absolute ranges and numbers**. T9 adds `sfdisk -J` when it is on `PATH`.

- **T1. The test disk's layout.**
  - The MBR slots are `0x83 {2048, 260096}`, `0x06 {262144, 32768}`, `0x83 {294912, 8192}` and `0x05 {303104, 28672}`.
  - EBR 1 at 303104: `0x83 {2048, 8192}`, link `0x05 {10240, 18432}`.
  - EBR 2 at 313344: `0x83 {2048, 16384}`.
  - Expected: `1:{2048,260096} 2:{262144,32768} 3:{294912,8192} 5:{305152,8192} 6:{315392,16384}`. The container is not listed and there is no number 4.
- **T2. Offset conventions.** Three logicals placed so that swapping the two conventions changes the absolute starts. Literal expectations.
- **T3. Loops.** Each case lists the logicals before the loop and reads at most 64 sectors (counted):
  - a link to itself;
  - a link back to EBR 1;
  - a link to an earlier EBR;
  - a 1000-EBR forward chain, which stops at 64 reads with `ranges` full.
- **T4. Bad logicals:** past the container, past the disk, count 0, first sector equal to its EBR, type 0x07, an extended type. Each is skipped, and a later valid logical is found.
- **T5. Bad links:** outside the container, type 0x83, count 0, a missing signature, a read error. Each ends the chain and keeps what was found.
- **T6. Numbering with gaps and limits.**
  - An empty primary slot 2: numbers 1 and 3, then 5….
  - 3 primaries and 14 logicals into `ranges[16]`: 16 listed, numbered 1–3 and 5–17. Number 17 is listed but unreachable as a volume.
  - `ranges[4]` gives 4 entries.
- **T7. Several extended entries:** only the first is walked.
- **T8. GPT and sector size.**
  - A protective MBR with a 0x05 entry still gives GPT's list.
  - GPT numbers follow entry indexes, with gaps for empty entries.
  - 4096-byte sectors give T1's ranges ÷ 8.
- **T9. Optional:** `sfdisk -J` on T1's image lists the same numbers, starts and sizes. `SKIP` without `sfdisk`.

### Step 2 (`tests/unit/kernel/filesystem/`, MBR in-memory disks)

- **T10. Two volumes of one kind** (one `TEST_CASE` each for ext2, ext3 and ext4, as `vda3` and `vda4`):
  1. Both mount; `/proc/mounts` shows both.
  2. Each reads back its own file, and the `st_dev`s differ.
  3. Every `on_write` sector falls in its own partition's range.
  4. A second mount of a volume → `Busy`; a wrong kind → `NotFound`.
- **T11. Independent journals** (ext4).
  - Unmounting `/a` clears only `a`'s `needs_recovery`.
  - `SyncPeriodic` puts `/b/p` on a fresh-core copy taken after it.
  - `DetachAll` leaves both clean.
- **T12. Identity.** The two images are identical, so the same inode number exists on both volumes. With `/a/hello.txt` open for writing:
  - `Remove("/b/hello.txt")` → 0;
  - `Remove("/a/hello.txt")` → `EBUSY`;
  - `Remount("/b", ro)` → 0;
  - `Remount("/a", ro)` → `EBUSY`.
- **T13. Across volumes.** `Rename`/`Link` from `/a` to `/b` → `EXDEV`, with no write.
- **T14. Fault isolation.** Writes fail in `vda4` only: `/b` gives `EIO` and turns `ro`; `/a` stays `rw` and unmounts clean.
- **T15. The root's own type.** With `vda3` ext4 as the root, `vda4` ext4 mounts; `vda3` elsewhere → `Busy`; `DetachAll` leaves both clean.
- **T16. Sixteen partitions** (GPT, 17 FAT16 entries):
  - `vda2`–`vda11` mount, and the next mount → `Full`;
  - after an unmount, `vda16` → `Done`;

- **T17. Rescan.** `/a` is kept and still reads. A slot whose kind changed is re-registered under the new kind, and a vanished slot is forgotten.
- **T18. Options per kind**, also after a slot changes kind.
- **T18b** (`mount_table_tests.cpp`): 21 registrations succeed. A second name for a driver fails, and the old name still resolves.

### Step 5 (`concurrency_tests.cpp`, `[concurrency]`, under ASan in `unit-test` and TSan in `unit-test-tsan`)

The setup is T10's disk, with ext4 at `/a` and `/b`, plus `/tmp`.

- **T23. Progress across volumes.**
  1. A disk hook parks thread A's write into `vda3` on a latch, so A holds `/a`'s lock.
  2. Thread B, within 5 s each:
     - works on `/b`;
     - lists `/`;
     - writes `/tmp`;
     - reads `/proc/mounts`.
  3. A `SyncPeriodic` thread must still be waiting.
  4. Control: thread C writing `/a/y` must not finish while A is parked.

  **Mutations:** one shared lock (B times out); no lock (C finishes early).
- **T24. Pinning.** `Read` in a loop on one descriptor while another thread closes and reopens it, 5000 rounds: bytes or `EBADF`, with no ASan or TSan report.
- **T25. Compound atomicity.** A's `Open("/a/f", write)` is parked inside the open. B's `Remove("/a/f")` must still be waiting after 200 ms. After the release, B gets `EBUSY` while A's descriptor is open.
- **T26. One-lock rule.** `Files::Testing::HoldVolume("/a")`, then `Files::Stat("/b/x")`: the violation counter goes from 0 to 1.
- **T27. Per-task errors.** Interleaved `EROFS` and `ENOENT` failures on two threads each read back their own error.
- **T28. Stress.**
  - 4 workers × 3000 seeded operations on `/a`, `/b` and `/tmp`;
  - meanwhile `SyncPeriodic`, `/proc/mounts` reads, and `Remount("/b")` churn (`EBUSY` accepted).

  Afterwards:
  - each file holds its owner's last write, through `Files` and through a fresh-core `MountCopy` after `DetachAll`;
  - neither volume needs recovery;
  - the violation counter is 0;
  - no ASan or TSan reports.
- **T29. QEMU, 4 CPUs** (STORAGE shell, boot 1).
  1. `cp /bin/<200 KiB+ program> /data/c1 & cp … /c2 & write /tmp/t x & wait`.
  2. The harness checks `/c1` and `/c2` with `debugfs cat` against the build's bytes.
  3. `e2fsck -fn` is clean on both volumes.
  4. No lock-order panic.

## 6. Risks for the implementer

- **ext2 is renamed and moved.** Grep for `vda4` and for literal sectors in `tools/`, `src/kernel/kernel_test.cpp`, `tools/matrix.py` and the docs.
- **Host test support under threads.** The scheduler stubs, host heap, disk hooks and `KLogger` (not locked) must be thread-safe or unused on threaded paths.
- **`Resolved::Walk` and `ProgramFile`** relied on the global lock; both follow D17 now.
- **The `Close` split** has a window in which `Remove` sees the file as still open (`EBUSY`). That is acceptable.
- **The data volume sits inside the container,** and the free 4 MiB tail stays clear of GPT's backup table.
